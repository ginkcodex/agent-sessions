# RF-33 — Publish fake ACP hold turns atomically

- **Status:** ✅ Done
- **Estimate:** 1 h
- **Depends on:** None
- **Plan:** [review-fixes.md](../../plan/review-fixes.md)

## Scope

Investigate the macOS CI `TestChatSendNowSteers` timeout from run 43690937665.
The fake ACP agent publishes `turns[sessionID]` before registering the `hold`
turn's steering channel. Steering in that gap reports `injected` and sends its
chunk, but cannot signal the hold turn to finish. Publish both under one lock.
Keep production chat behavior unchanged; file-search failures are out of scope.

## Likely files

- `internal/acp/acptest/agent.go`
- `internal/acp/acptest/agent_test.go`

## Acceptance criteria and tests

- A running `hold` or `askhold` turn always has its steering channel registered.
- A regression exercises concurrent prompt registration and steering, including
  the invariant that previously allowed an injected steer to leave a turn hung.
- ACP, engine chat and RPC chat tests pass, with repeated steering and race checks.
- No shared indexes are edited; registration belongs to the coordinator.

## Evidence

Investigation: `/tmp/as-ci-43690937665.log:134-135` shows a steering chunk and
`steered`, but no `turn_end`. `acptest.agent.prompt` registers the cancel function
under `mu`, then later registers the hold channel under a second lock;
`agent.steer` can see a running turn with a nil channel in between. The engine
correctly waits for the prompt reply, which never arrives in that interleaving.

Fix: create the buffered steering signal before publishing a hold/askhold turn,
register both maps under the same lock, and remove both together on return.
No production engine or protocol changes, timeouts, sleeps, or dependencies added.

Regression: `TestHoldRegistrationAndSteering` races registration with a locked
observer across 1,000 turns, checks that a published hold always has its signal,
then injects steering and requires `end_turn`. Before the fix it failed in all
10 runs (at iterations 1–149); after the fix it passes repeated and race runs.
This is a scheduling stress regression, not a forced-interleaving hook.
